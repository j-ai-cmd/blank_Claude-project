# The three formats

Researched 2026-10-12 from public, logged-out Instagram pages. Use these for structure and angle only. Never copy their content or visuals.

## A. What-if scenario (from @zenith.whatif, 64K followers, posts daily)

**Shape:** a huge "what if" premise, then a timeline that keeps jumping forward: 1 second, 1 hour, 1 day, 1 year, 10,000 years. Each jump is worse or weirder than the last. Real numbers keep it credible ("8 min 19 sec", "1,037 mph"). It ends on a survival or choice question.

**Proof:** "Every human disappeared" got 1M likes and 5.6K comments. "Rain for a year" got 181K. Huge earth-scale stakes beat everything else. The gender what-ifs got few likes but lots of comments relative to likes.

**Comment trigger:** "Where would you go?" / "How long would you last?" / "What would you miss first?"

**Sherlock version:** "What if [AI event] happened tomorrow?" Holmes walks through the timeline as a chain of deductions. Kit visuals: a timeline component, counters, a clock that ticks forward.
Examples: what if AI wrote all code, what if GPUs vanished, what if every AI said "no" for a day, what if open-source AI were banned.

## B. Under-the-hood teardown (from @speedy_devv, 111K followers)

**Shape:** "Let's design **X**." Dark diagrams build X one layer at a time, with a live counter ("1,000,000 new databases / week"). One idea per beat.

**Proof:** the PS5 emulator open-source reel got 68K likes and 2.3K comments. Under-the-hood explainers (Supabase, Vercel, quantization) bring steady followers. AI drama (PewDiePie's uncensored model) got 17K.

**Comment trigger:** "Comment WORD and I'll send you the link/repo/paper." On one reel this got 2,029 comments on 2,384 likes, almost one comment per like. "Follow for more" closes the rest.

**Sherlock version:** "How does X really work?" is the case. The clues are the layers, and the verdict is the one-line truth. Add a controversial edge where there is one: "Did Anthropic quantize its own model to save money?"

## C. AI-coding sitcom bit (from @canwebuildthis, 4.9K followers but 26K-like reels)

**Shape:** one relatable moment between a builder and AI tools that act like characters. It opens on a dialogue card ("Claude. Are you sure?"), with a punchline in under 20s and a short, dry, lowercase caption.

**Proof:** "Claude vs Codex: one asks four questions, one asks none" got 26K likes. "She said no sixty-one times" (making the AI redo things) got 18K. The account reaches far past its followers. Feelings and office-humor posts did much worse; tool personalities and coder pain do best.

**Comment trigger:** "Which one are you working with?" / "What's the pettiest thing you've made your AI redo?" People pick sides.

**Sherlock version:** Holmes deduces an AI's personality from how it answers ("Codex asks nothing. Claude asks four questions. Observe the difference.") Short, 20–30s, a chat-bubble component.

## What all three share

1. The hook is a question or premise in the first 2s, with on-screen text.
2. There are two sides to argue (which tool, which future, run or stay).
3. The caption ends with one question aimed at the comments.
4. They post often (daily or more).
